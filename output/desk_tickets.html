Ticket 101 — Tauhid Zaman, "Bulldog tee"

customer_order · CC-TEE-WHITE, size S, qty 1 · linked to invoice 501

What the database says

  • Classic Bulldog Tee, size S: 0 in stock (Aisle B). Other sizes: M 5, L 3, XL 2.
  • Invoice 501: $840 to Bulldog Print Co, "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S", due 2026-08-28, still open. That's 3 days overdue on the desk date.
  • Bulldog Print Co is the only apparel vendor (apparel reprint, 5 lead days). Because invoice 501 is unpaid, the shop can't order from it.
  • Checking has $3,400, and no payments have been made yet.

Expected

Who the Boss should call first, and why

Inventory. This is a customer order for one specific item, so the first question is whether we actually have it. Inventory is the only agent that owns stock and vendor data, and the ticket already gives it everything it needs: SKU, size and quantity.

Step by step

  1. Boss gets ready. It calls get_shop_date (2026-08-31, used for every "overdue" check) and get_ticket(101) to read the SKU, size, qty and the linked invoice_id 501.
  2. Boss → Inventory: "Is CC-TEE-WHITE size S in stock for 1 unit? If not, who could restock it and can we order from them?"
    • check_stock("CC-TEE-WHITE", "S") shows 0. A second check_stock without a size shows the sizes that are in stock.
    • list_vendors shows Bulldog Print Co is the right vendor (apparel reprint, 5 lead days), but can_order is false because of unpaid invoice 501.
    • Inventory should not draft a restock order. It should report the blocker to the Boss.
  3. Boss → Accounting: the ticket links to invoice 501, and that unpaid invoice is exactly what blocks the restock.
    • list_unpaid_invoices confirms $840, due 2026-08-28, 3 days overdue.
    • get_cash_and_payments shows $3,400 in checking and confirms 501 hasn't been paid.
    • Accounting should include the rent from ticket 102 in its cash check: $3,400 − $2,400 − $840 = $160 left if both are paid.
    • Paying 501 is my decision. If the Boss asks Accounting to pay it, Accounting can only propose_payment. Nothing is paid until I approve it in the dashboard.
  4. Boss → Customer Service: the Boss passes on the confirmed facts, and Customer Service drafts the reply with draft_customer_reply(101).
    • The draft tells Tauhid that size S is out of stock, offers the sizes we do have, and gives no restock date, since the vendor is blocked.
    • It never mentions invoices, vendors or costs.
    • Customer Service shouldn't need to ask Inventory again, because the Boss already gave it the facts.
  5. Boss writes the report: ticket 101 is waiting_on_human.

Who stays out

Facilities: no lease or landlord is involved in this ticket.

Ticket 102 — Elm City Properties, "Rent due"

rent_notice · lease 1 · "Email: shop rent due in 2 days."

What the database says

  • Lease 1: Chapel Street shop, landlord Elm City Properties, $2,400/month, next due 2026-09-02. That's 2 days after the desk date, so it matches the email.
  • Checking has $3,400, and no payments have been made yet.
  • Another bill wants the same cash: invoice 501, $840 and overdue (see ticket 101).

Expected

Who the Boss should call first, and why

Facilities. A rent notice is about the lease, and before any money moves someone has to check the email is real. The sender must be our landlord, and the amount and due date must match the lease. Facilities owns the lease, and it's the agent that hands the payment to Accounting. So for this ticket the Boss needs only one delegation.

Step by step

  1. Boss gets ready. It calls get_shop_date and get_ticket(102) to get lease_id 1.
  2. Boss → Facilities: "Verify the rent notice for lease 1 and arrange the payment."
    • get_ticket(102) and get_rent_and_cash(1) check three things: the requester matches the landlord on the lease, the rent is $2,400, and 2026-09-02 is 2 days after 2026-08-31.
    • The email has no amount, so Facilities uses the lease amount and never a number from an email.
    • If anything didn't match, Facilities should stop and flag possible fraud.
  3. Facilities → Accounting (a peer delegation the Boss doesn't have to do): "Request the $2,400 rent payment for lease 1."
    • get_cash_and_payments shows $3,400 in checking and confirms the rent hasn't been paid already.
    • list_unpaid_invoices shows the $840 bill too. $2,400 + $840 = $3,240, which fits within $3,400.
    • Accounting calls propose_payment(rent, lease 1, $2,400, checking). That only creates a proposal, which waits for my approval in the dashboard.
    • If I approve, checking goes to $1,000 and the lease's next due date moves to 2026-10-02.
    • If I deny it, or I'm not there, nothing is paid.
  4. Facilities drafts the landlord reply with draft_customer_reply(102).
    • The draft confirms $2,400 due September 2.
    • It says "paid" only if the payment actually went through. Otherwise it says the payment is in internal approval.
    • It never mentions our cash balance or other bills.
  5. Boss writes the report: waiting_on_human until I approve the payment.

Who stays out

Inventory and Customer Service: no products are involved, and Facilities writes the landlord reply itself.

Across the 7 runs: 103 model calls, 127 tool calls, about 281k input and 24k output tokens. All four approvals were signed "Madina Muratova (via Claude Code)".

1. Agent performance

Ticket 101: good reasoning, but slow and a bit leaky

What worked:

  • In run 1 the Boss sent Inventory and Accounting out in the same turn. It gave them the exact SKU, size, qty and invoice_id 501, so nobody had to guess.
  • Accounting made the connection I cared about most. The tee is out of stock because Bulldog Print Co is blocked by invoice 501 ($840.00, 3 days overdue on the desk date). It proposed paying it (#1) and noted checking would still have $2,560.00, enough for the $2,400 rent.
  • Once I approved #1, Inventory saw that Bulldog Print Co could be ordered from again. It proposed buying exactly the shortfall of 1 tee for $8.00 (#2), with 2026-09-05 as the earliest delivery.
  • The final reply (draft #8) was careful. It said a tee is approved and that September 5 is "only an estimate", which is honest.

What could have been better:

  • Slow. It took three runs (57 s, 234 s and 373 s). In runs 2 and 3, Accounting was asked again just to confirm that payment #1 existed, which repeated work nothing had changed.
  • A leaky draft. Customer Service's run-2 draft (#6) told Tauhid that "a purchase proposal is pending human approval" and used the SKU code "CC-TEE-WHITE". That's internal detail a customer shouldn't see.
  • A possible double order. Invoice 501's own description is "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S". So we paid for a reprint of this exact tee and bought one more. Neither the agents nor I can tell whether the first reprint will arrive, because nothing in the database tracks deliveries. Inventory for size S is still 0.
  • It needed fixing to work at all. In a practice run before the real one, this ticket got stuck. The Boss wrote "Authorize or decline restocking" as a note instead of asking Inventory for a proposal I could approve, so we had to add that routing rule.

Ticket 102: the cleanest ticket

What worked:

  • Run 1 followed my plan exactly: Boss → Facilities → Accounting.
  • Facilities checked that the requester (Elm City Properties) matched the landlord on lease 1, and that $2,400.00 was due 2026-09-02, before involving Accounting. That's the fraud check I wanted.
  • Accounting proposed #3 and spelled out that checking would drop to $152.00.
  • In run 2, Facilities noticed that the lease's next_due had moved to 2026-10-02, because approving the payment advances it. It didn't call the mismatch a scam, so the Problem 7 exception rule did its job.
  • Only the second landlord draft (#10) says the rent "has been paid", and that's true by then.

What could have been better:

  • In run 2 the Boss and Facilities both delegated to Accounting with the same question (was #3 paid?). Accounting therefore called get_shop_date, get_cash_and_payments and list_proposals twice each.
  • Nobody flagged what paying the rent did to the future. Only $152.00 is left, and the next $2,400 rent is due 2026-10-02. Accounting only compares a payment with today's balance.

Ticket 103: honest about limits, but the customer got less than they asked for

What worked:

  • Inventory found 8 of the 20 size M hoodies on hand, at $22 cost and $58 list price.
  • It tried to restock the other 12 ($264.00), and the server refused because checking only had $152.00. The Boss reported that $112 gap honestly instead of promising a date.
  • Inventory proposed price override #4: $52.00 for the 8 on hand. That's 10.34% off, with $30.00 margin per hoodie, so it's above cost as the rule requires.
  • After I approved it, the final reply (draft #13) quoted $52.00 for 8 and made no promise about the other 12.
  • Accounting and Facilities stayed out, which was right.

What could have been better:

  • In run 2, Inventory tried propose_purchase again even though cash hadn't changed, and it was refused again.
  • Customer Service wrote two drafts in the same run (#12, then #13). That run used 51,718 input tokens, the most of any run.
  • No hoodie reply offered other sizes, even though size L had 14 in stock and the prompt allowed it.
  • In the end the Yale AI Club gets 8 of 20 hoodies, mainly because the rent for ticket 102 had already used the cash.

2. Expected vs. actual

Ticket 101

  • Who went first: I expected the Boss to call Inventory first, then Accounting, then Customer Service. In reality, Inventory and Accounting were delegated in the same turn, with Customer Service after both. Facilities stayed out, as I planned.
  • Tools: every tool I listed was used. The extras were get_pricing (I'd called it optional), propose_payment and propose_purchase.
  • What I expected: one pass ending "waiting on human", with paying 501 as my decision.
  • What happened: three runs ending resolved. Each approval unlocked the next step, and the $8.00 restock wasn't in my plan at all.

Ticket 102

  • Delegations: I planned a single Boss → Facilities delegation, with Facilities passing the payment to Accounting. Run 1 matched exactly.
  • Tools: the planned set and nothing extra beyond the bookkeeping tools.
  • The one difference: in run 2 the Boss also went straight to Accounting, so Accounting was asked the same question twice.
  • The lease date: my plan predicted that approval would move the lease to 2026-10-02. It did, and the agents coped with it.

Ticket 103

  • Who worked: Inventory then Customer Service, with no Accounting, as I planned.
  • The blocker changed. I assumed the restock would be blocked by invoice 501. By the time 103 ran, 501 had been paid during ticket 101, so the vendor was open and the real blocker became cash ($152.00 against $264.00). I planned each ticket on its own, but they share one checking account and one vendor list.
  • New tools: Inventory used propose_purchase (refused, no money moved) and propose_price_override, a tool that didn't exist when I wrote the plan.
  • The order of work: run 2 went Boss → Customer Service → Inventory → Customer Service.

The biggest overall difference: my plan pictured one Boss pass per ticket. In practice every human decision needed another run, and each run rebuilt its picture from scratch. That's a large part of why 3 tickets took 103 model calls.

3. One agent vs. multiple agents

Honestly, a lot of this could have been one agent with the same MCP tools:

  • Ticket 102 is really four tool calls: get_rent_and_cash, get_cash_and_payments, propose_payment and draft_customer_reply. With three agents it took 15 model calls per run and still duplicated work, since Accounting answered the same question twice in run 2. One agent would have been cheaper and harder to confuse.
  • The re-runs. Run 3 of ticket 101 used 13 model calls and 21 tool calls to confirm two things that were already recorded, and then call resolve_ticket. A single agent that remembered where it stopped could simply continue after my approval.
  • Plain lookups like check_stock and get_pricing don't need a specialist. Customer Service already had both tools, so the Boss relaying Inventory's numbers to Customer Service was overhead.

Where the team did earn its keep was separation of duties:

  • Customer Service never sees invoices or cash, so the reply to Tauhid couldn't mention the overdue invoice.
  • Only Accounting can propose payments, and only Inventory can propose purchases and prices.
  • Facilities checks the landlord before anyone proposes $2,400.

That mirrors real internal controls. But the strongest protections in our run came from the MCP server, not from having many agents:

  • the propose → approve split;
  • the human key;
  • the cash check that refused the $264 hoodie restock.

Those would protect a single agent just as well. For three tickets I think one well-prompted agent plus these server rules would have been enough. A team makes more sense when there are many tickets at once and each role needs a long, specialised prompt.

4. Three problems the team could solve with the tools we already built

  1. A customer wants a Crest Mug.
    • check_stock shows CC-MUG-CREST at 0.
    • list_vendors shows Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead time) with no unpaid invoices.
    • Inventory can propose_purchase at the $3.50 unit cost. Even with only $152.00 left, a small restock fits, and the server would refuse anything bigger.
    • After I approve, the order is drafted with an earliest delivery of 2026-09-03, and Customer Service replies at the $14.00 list price.
  2. A suspicious rent notice. Say someone other than Elm City Properties emails asking for this month's rent at a different amount.
    • Facilities' get_ticket + get_rent_and_cash check would catch that the requester or amount doesn't match lease 1, and it would stop without involving Accounting.
    • If the real landlord sends the October notice, Accounting would try propose_payment for $2,400. The server would refuse, because checking has $152.00, and Accounting would report the shortfall. That isn't a happy ending, but it's the correct one.
  3. A club asks for 30 Yale caps at a discount.
    • CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 on hand, with $6.00 cost and $22.00 list price.
    • Inventory can confirm stock and propose a price override above $6.00, which I decide on a slip.
    • Customer Service then quotes the decided price. No cash moves and no restock is needed.

One catch for all three: the problem must already exist as a row in tickets. There's no tool for creating tickets (see 5c).

5. Three problems the team could not solve yet

  1. Tracking orders and deliveries.
    • The gap: purchase #2 was paid ($8.00), but tee size S is still 0. Nothing records when or whether it arrives, and nobody can tell if invoice 501's "rush reprint" was the same tee. The shop could easily pay twice or promise stock that never comes.
    • What we'd need:
      • a purchase_orders table: vendor, SKU, size, qty, status, expected and received dates, and linked invoice and payment;
      • a receive_delivery tool that increases inventory after a human confirms the delivery;
      • a rule that Inventory checks open orders before proposing a new purchase.
  2. Money coming in and planning ahead.
    • The gap: revenue isn't modeled. The Yale AI Club's 8 hoodies at $52.00 ($416.00) can't be recorded, inventory doesn't drop when something sells, and checking only goes down (it ended at $152.00) while $2,400 rent is due 2026-10-02. The desk date is also frozen at 2026-08-31, so "overdue" can never change.
    • What we'd need:
      • a sales table and a human-confirmed record_sale tool that lowers stock and adds cash;
      • a cash-forecast tool that lists upcoming rent and invoices against the balance;
      • an Accounting rule that reserves next month's rent before approving purchases;
      • a way to advance desk.date_today.
  3. Talking to the outside world.
    • The gap: tickets only exist if someone inserts them, requesters have names but no email addresses, and every reply stays a draft. Drafts #5–#13 are just sitting in output/drafts.json. If the Yale AI Club writes back "we'll take the 8 at $52", nothing would reopen or update ticket 103.
    • What we'd need:
      • a contacts table;
      • an intake tool that turns incoming emails into tickets;
      • a send_message tool behind human approval, with a sent log;
      • ticket threads, so replies attach to the right ticket and the Boss can act on them.

What I take away

The agents were good at the parts where the data was clear: matching the landlord to the lease, linking the out-of-stock tee to invoice 501, and keeping the discount above cost. They were weakest at coordination: repeated checks, double drafts, and internal wording leaking into a customer email. The most valuable design choice was making money a proposal that only a human can turn into a payment. Every dollar that left the account ($840.00 + $8.00 + $2,400.00 = $3,248.00, from $3,400.00 down to $152.00) went through a slip I signed. And the one purchase the shop couldn't afford was stopped by the server, not by an agent's good judgment.
